01 / OVERVIEW

Refinement at two levels.

A capable robot needs both informative representations and an effective connection between understanding and action.

Axiom-0 couples two complementary mechanisms within a VLM–DiT policy. Intra-VLM Representation Refinement (IVRR) supervises intermediate features during training. VLM–Action Interface Refinement (VAIR) recursively refines the conditioning used by the action generator. Selective joint optimization connects the learned representations to their downstream use.

The result is a refine-before-act policy: improve the information available to the model, then refine that information before generating an action chunk.

02 / METHOD

From representation to action.

Training-time supervision meets
inference-time refinement.

01 TRAINING

IVRR

Intra-VLM Representation Refinement

Align selected intermediate VLM features with spatial reference features through learned projections, jointly with action learning.

The auxiliary alignment branch is used only during training.

02 BEFORE ACTION GENERATION

VAIR

VLM–Action Interface Refinement

Recursively update conditioning values with a shared refinement module, then reuse the refined cache throughout action sampling.

The deployed policy refines conditioning with fixed parameters.

Axiom-0 uses the VLM backbone and DiT action-generator architecture of Xiaomi-Robotics-1, initialized from its public checkpoint. Subsequent RoboCasa-specific training uses only pretrain_human300, with no target-50 demonstrations or evaluation trajectories. Full training and implementation details are provided in the report.

06 / LOOKING AHEAD

A first step toward
safe recursive self-improvement.

Our long-term objective is a robot that improves from experience while preserving dependable behavior. Execution feedback should help identify limitations, guide candidate updates, and provide evidence for deciding whether those updates should be adopted.

Axiom-0 takes an initial step through self-refinement before acting. It uses fixed parameters at deployment; autonomous policy revision and validation remain future research directions.
